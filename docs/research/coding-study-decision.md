## Recommended decision rule

Keep the article moving through the literature synthesis. If only the author can commit, choose a clearly labeled personal case study. If several developers can commit to the same measurement process, choose a small feasibility pilot first. Treat a confirmatory study as a separate design decision requiring a justified sample size; there is no participant-count shortcut to broad claims.

| Access available | Choose | What must be available | What the article can claim |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Only the author is confirmed | Optional personal case study | A real time commitment, distinct suitable tasks, a frozen AI/no-AI assignment, and effort/quality logging | Exploratory observations about this developer, tasks, repository, and tool configuration |
| A few developers are interested but unconfirmed | Keep recruitment exploratory; retain the personal-case or literature-only route | Confirm actual slots and task/review access before designing around them | No participant-study findings until commitments and observations exist |
| Several developers are confirmed | Small multi-developer feasibility pilot | Each can perform both assigned conditions; suitable task/repository access, comparable logging, and enough review capacity | Feasibility, missingness, failure modes, and exploratory variation; not a powered population estimate by default |
| Broader study is feasible after the pilot | Separately planned confirmatory study | Defined target population, sampling frame, estimand, practical effect threshold, frozen design/analysis, and dependence-aware sample-size justification | An estimate bounded by the studied population and setting, with explicitly justified uncertainty |

If effort or task access cannot be measured reliably, use literature synthesis alone rather than forcing an anecdote into an experiment. Participant interest is not a commitment. ## Design rules shared by the routes

- Use distinct comparable tasks, randomized within developer and balanced across order/period where feasible. Do not have someone solve the same task twice. Standardize tool warm-up and record prior task familiarity.
- Measure total delivery effort: author implementation/debugging, reviewer effort, and correction/rework. Keep waiting and calendar time separate from active labor. Record assistance separately from final review.
- Freeze acceptance checks and task stopping rules before assignment. Retain timeouts, abandonment, defects, and rejected changes in the denominator. Reporting time only for completed tasks can hide failures.
- Blind final reviewers to condition where practical and record when blinding fails. Record identity overlap rather than asserting independence from an ID string.
- Repeated tasks do not create independent developers. Related task variants also remain dependent; a future main-study analysis must account for developer and underlying-case structure. A personal case cannot establish a population-wide productivity effect.
