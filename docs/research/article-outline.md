## Editorial contract

Write a literature-led first draft, approximately 2,500–3,000 words. Ask which claims survive clear measurement, realistic costs, and a valid information set. Let the evidence determine whether a promise is overhyped; do not assume a negative verdict.

The current source set supports narrow qualitative discussion. Numerical candidates marked `HOLD-NUM` need admission work first. Proposed tests and hypothetical examples must be labeled as such. Original empirical results, current model rankings, and independent-verification claims are unavailable.

## Opening — What belongs in the denominator? (~200 words)

- Introduce four outputs: an agent's completed task, a team of agents' report, a code patch, and a historical trading signal.
- Identify the corresponding questions: repeatability and supervision; total resources and review; accepted delivery effort; time-valid information and execution.
- Frame “overhyped” as a gap between the promise and the demonstrated result in a specified setting.
- **Claim anchors:** AIH-A02, AIH-M04, AIH-C05, AIH-F03/F04.
- **Avoid:** stating that every technology is ineffective, or claiming our test apparatus has produced model findings.

## Autonomous agents — Who owns the checking? (~500 words)

1. Explain the time-horizon definition before discussing any capability claim. Human task difficulty and probability thresholds do not measure unattended reliability or labor saved.
2. Describe multidimensional reliability evaluation and its studied-setting limits.
3. Explain our proposed supervision contrast, including failed attempts and final verification effort in cost accounting.
4. State the countercase: bounded, easily verified workflows may be useful even when broad autonomy claims are premature.

**Evidence anchors:** AIH-A01/A02; proposed method AIH-A03. Sources S-A01–S-A04. First-draft wording can stay qualitative. G01/G05 must close before model-specific accuracy/horizon figures appear.

**Future figure:** verified completion versus total cost and supervision effort, from real repeated attempts. A software-test pass rate is not an acceptable substitute.

## Multi-agent systems — Does the architecture earn its cost? (~500 words)

1. Separate naturally parallel tasks from dependent sequential work.
2. Present both favorable and unfavorable cases from the same controlled study; preserve the resource and task scope.
3. Explain why architecture-selection accuracy is a different target from workflow completion.
4. Introduce our comparison with single-agent self-review; count coordination, repeated input, aggregation, and verification within total resources.

**Evidence anchors:** AIH-M01/M02; version gate AIH-M03; proposed method AIH-M04. Sources S-M01–S-M03 are one evidence family, not three replications.

**Numerical hold:** do not quote relative performance endpoints or the architecture-selection percentage until G02 resolves version, denominator, and uncertainty. Do not average task-specific extremes into a “swarm effect.”

**Future figure:** paired architecture results split by task structure; additional budget-response curves require trials at multiple declared budgets.

## Coding productivity — Faster generation, accepted output, and value (~550 words)

1. Present the maintenance-task experiment and enterprise field experiments as different settings with different outcomes.
2. Distinguish time per task from output per period, and access assignment from tool adoption. The results cannot be combined by subtracting their headline percentages.
3. Include METR's follow-up selection/measurement caveats so the early slowdown does not become a permanent verdict.
4. Separate survey usefulness from randomized measured effects.
5. Explain the selected one-participant personal case study, separating author verification from independent review. The [promoted session](coding-session-ready.md) is technically ready: task bank, Codex/time/timer, common runtime, no-prior-implementation self-report, and one authorized task-free availability receipt are bound; the original two-stage schedule is unchanged. Warm-up and per-attempt operator attestations remain, independent review is unconfirmed, and no coding observations exist. The generic `READY` response is setup evidence only; it does not measure coding productivity or authorize further provider calls.

**Evidence anchors:** AIH-C01–C05. Positive enterprise evidence is part of the section, not a footnote to a predetermined negative story.

**Numerical hold:** exact effects, sample/period detail, estimands, intervals, and occupation breakdowns need G03/G04. Do not label a reported SE as a CI or invent a confidence level.

**Possible figure after admission:** two separately labeled study panels with their own outcome axes and uncertainty conventions. Do not draw a common-axis meta-analysis. Our own implementation/review/rework chart requires participant observations.

## Trading signals — What did the model know, and when? (~650 words)

1. Separate the prompt's historical date from the information a model may contain.
2. Describe lookahead diagnostics and vintage/release-time examples in their studied settings.
3. Specify our proposed earnings-release test: frozen inputs/model, prospective logging, fixed entry/exit windows, simple baselines, and auditable timing.
4. Separate probability-forecast skill from event-payoff economics and from factor-adjusted alpha.
5. Report what would change the view: an incrementally useful prospective signal after realistic costs, with sufficient observations and dependence-aware uncertainty.

**Evidence anchors:** AIH-F01/F02; proposed methods/status AIH-F03/F04. Sources S-F01/S-F02 identify evaluation risks, not a verdict on every language-model strategy.

**Numerical hold:** contamination magnitudes, strategy returns, Sharpe ratios, and error rates are unavailable or require G06. No real prospective prediction series exists. The synthetic integration and local test CA must not supply article result figures.

**Future figure:** prospective predictive performance and cost/coverage sensitivities. Any historical comparison must identify periods and cannot attribute differences solely to contamination.

## Closing — What would justify the promise? (~200 words)

Return to explicit criteria: dependable accepted outcomes, total effort and cost, appropriate baselines, and a time-valid information set. State the strongest demonstrated finding for each setting after admission, preserve counterevidence, and identify remaining uncertainty.

The current draft should end with an experiment plan and a transparent evidence boundary, not a claim that the experiments have already resolved the debate.
