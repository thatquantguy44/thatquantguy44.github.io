Useful alpha research does not always require a precise forecast of each asset's future return. A signal may still help if it ranks opportunities better than a baseline, identifies when information is more relevant, or improves how a portfolio turns a view into a trade.

Areas worth exploring include:

- **Relative information:** cross-sectional rankings or changes in conviction can be more decision-relevant than exact point forecasts.
- **Conditional signals:** liquidity, volatility, horizon, or event context can turn a broad hypothesis into a more specific one to test.
- **Portfolio implementation:** turnover, costs, borrow, capacity, and constraints can determine whether a modest signal remains useful after portfolio construction.
- **Research design:** point-in-time data and robust validation help distinguish persistent information from leakage or a convenient backtest artifact.

None of these is a shortcut to returns. They shift the research question from *"Can I predict the future precisely?"* to *"What decision changes, and does that change hold up after risk, costs, and constraints?"*
